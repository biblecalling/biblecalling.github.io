How to Pray When You Don't Have the Words

This is the most common worry we hear, and the answer is simpler than people expect. There is no required formula or special vocabulary:

  • Say what is true: “I'm scared.” “I'm angry.” “I don't know what to ask for.” Honest prayers are the ones the Psalms are full of. Many of them are raw complaints, and they are still prayers.
  • Name the person or the situation: Specific prayers are easier to keep praying than vague generalities.
  • Ask for what you actually need: That might be physical healing, but it might also be emotional calm, patience with family, or simply the stamina to get through today.
  • Stop and stay quiet for a minute: Prayer is not only talking. Many people find the silence afterward is where true peace and settling happen.
  • Close with something simple: A quiet “Amen” or “Thank you for listening, Lord.”

If you can only manage one sentence, “God, help me,” is a complete and accepted prayer.

Why People Turn to Prayer in Hard Times

Prayer has been part of how human beings face illness, fear, and loss for thousands of years. Scripture speaks to this directly:

Peace That Guards the Heart
“Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.”
— Philippians 4:6–7 (NIV)

The Apostle Paul writes in Philippians 4:6–7 that instead of being anxious, we should bring our requests to God and trust that His peace will guard our hearts. Similarly, Psalm 46:1 describes God as an ever-present refuge and help in times of trouble.

Prayer remains widespread today. The Pew Research Center's Religious Landscape Study found that 44% of American adults report praying daily. That also means many people searching for a prayer online are not daily pray-ers, which is why our guides never assume past religious experience or fluency.

A Grounded Perspective on Medical Care: We avoid making transactional guarantees about outcomes. Prayer is a sacred spiritual relationship, not a transaction, and we never claim it replaces professional medical care. If you or someone you love is ill, continue consulting doctors and specialists, and pray alongside that care.

A Note on Unanswered Prayers

Many visitors arrive at this page after praying for a long time without seeing the specific outcome they pleaded for. If that is your situation, you are not doing it wrong, and you have not failed in faith. Faithful believers throughout history have lived with that painful tension, and the biblical Psalms express that struggle openly. Keep praying, and keep talking with someone you trust — whether a pastor, counselor, or close friend.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a right time to pray?

No. Morning and bedtime are common habits because they bookend the day, but a two-sentence prayer whispered in a parking lot, on a commuter train, or in a hospital corridor counts just the same.

Do I need to pray out loud?

No. Silent prayer is just as valid as spoken prayer. Many people find that writing prayers down in a journal helps maintain focus.

Can I pray for someone who doesn't know I'm praying?

Yes. Believers routinely pray for friends, coworkers, family, and strangers without announcing it.

What if I'm not sure what I believe?

Many people pray first and work through their theology later. An honest, questioning prayer is always received with open arms.

Can I use a prayer that someone else wrote?

Yes. Using written prayers is a respected practice stretching back thousands of years. Make the words your own by inserting the specific names and details of your situation.

Where can I find prayers for urgent medical situations?

Start with our prayer for someone in the hospital, and always call emergency medical services immediately if anyone is in critical physical danger.
