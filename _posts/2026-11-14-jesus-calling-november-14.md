## Quick Answer

This companion reflection for **Jesus Calling November 14** explores the liberating, challenging nature of biblical forgiveness. Forgiveness is not pretending that injury never occurred, nor does it require staying in harm's way; it is the intentional release of the debt into God's hands, rooted in the grace Christ has given us.

## Reflection: Setting Down What Someone Else Did

Clinging to bitterness has often been compared to drinking poison while waiting for the other person to suffer; the one who holds onto resentment is the one who suffers the ongoing burn. Yet forgiveness is frequently misunderstood as superficial dismissal—as if forgiving someone means saying, *"It doesn't matter,"* or pretending the wound was insignificant.

Scripture gives us a far more robust, truth-filled vision. Biblical forgiveness never minimizes sin or excuses mistreatment. In Ephesians 4:32 and Colossians 3:13, the apostle Paul grounds human forgiveness in a prior reality: *"even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."* Notice that Paul does not begin with the offender's worthiness, their apology, or their remorse. He begins with the boundless, costly mercy God poured out on us when we were still helpless sinners. We forgive not because the other person deserves it, but because we ourselves have been forgiven an infinite debt.

Forgiveness also requires essential, biblical distinctions:
* **Forgiveness is not excusing:** It does not declare wrongdoing to be acceptable.
* **Forgiveness is distinct from trust:** Forgiveness is an act of grace granted freely; trust is relational confidence that must be rebuilt over time through proven repentance and consistency.
* **Forgiveness does not demand reconciliation with an unsafe person:** You can completely forgive someone before God while maintaining necessary, healthy physical or emotional boundaries. If you are experiencing abuse, harassment, or danger, your safety is paramount—reach out to trusted pastoral leaders, counselors, or local support authorities immediately. The victim is never responsible for the perpetrator's wrongdoing.

## Try This Today: A Gentle Forgiveness Practice

If you are carrying the heavy weight of an unresolved injury, bring it to the Lord with this quiet, deliberate exercise:

* **Name the Hurt Honestly:** On a private piece of paper, write the name of the person and the specific offense. Do not sanitize the pain; be completely honest before God.
* **Release the Ledger:** Pray a prayer of release: *"Lord, I choose to hand over this debt to You. I release my right to punish, avenge, or hold this against them. Deal with them in Your righteous justice and mercy."*
* **Protect Your Mind:** When the memory surfaces again, refuse to rehearse the grievance. Remind yourself: *"I have handed that debt over to God."*

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does forgiving someone mean I must remain in an unsafe relationship?
Absolutely not. Scripture never commands anyone to endure ongoing abuse, harm, or exploitation. Forgiveness is an internal release of vengeance before God; reconciliation requires two safe, repentant people. If you are in an unsafe situation, prioritize your physical and emotional safety by seeking immediate help from trusted authorities, medical professionals, or counselors.

### What if I forgive someone and the hurt comes back tomorrow?
Forgiveness is often both a decisive crisis moment and an ongoing daily practice. When painful memories or resentment resurface, treat it as an invitation to re-confirm your decision before God: *"Lord, I released this yesterday, and by Your grace, I release it again today."*
