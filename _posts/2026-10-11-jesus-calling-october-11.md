## What Is Jesus Calling October 11 About?

Jesus Calling October 11 centers on enjoying God's blessings without clinging to them possessively, finding our ultimate contentment in Christ's presence rather than created things. The reading highlights that while earthly pleasures are generous gifts from God, none is essential compared to the unshakeable presence of Jesus. Grounded in 1 Timothy 6:17 and James 1:17, the practical takeaway is to practice open-handed gratitude and thank the Giver above the gift.

## Today's Theme: Enjoying the Gifts While Clinging to the Giver

<p>
  The reading reminds believers that Jesus is the fulfillment of our deepest longings—the Alpha and Omega who satisfies the thirsty soul. Before knowing Him, people often look for lasting satisfaction in things that cannot sustain them. While God provides countless good gifts to enrich our lives, none of them can replace His presence. When we make gifts our ultimate source of security, fear of loss takes over. When we rest in Jesus, we are freed to enjoy His gifts without anxiety, bringing our hearts before God in <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/prayer/">prayers of thanksgiving</a>.
</p>

    &ldquo;My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him.&rdquo;

<p>As highlighted in our <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/bible/">Scripture guide</a>, the psalmist directs his core expectations away from fragile earthly systems and anchors them solely in the living Lord.</p>

### God gives richly for our enjoyment
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">1 Timothy 6:17 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 1 Timothy 6:17 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Scripture does not advocate ascetic misery. God is a generous Father who delights in blessing His children; the warning is against transferring our trust from the Giver to the gift.</p>

### Every perfect gift descends from above
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">James 1:17 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; James 1:17 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Recognizing that every blessing originated in God's kindness keeps our hearts humble and full of praise.</p>

## Why This Matters in Everyday Life

<p>
  There are two opposite ways to misuse God's blessings: cynical rejection or idolatrous worship. Scripture rejects both extremes. Holding blessings with open hands means we celebrate family, friendship, meaningful labor, and daily comforts with vibrant gratitude, yet we do not collapse if circumstances change. Our identity and security remain anchored in the one reality that can never be taken away: Christ in us, which we cultivate through a steady <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/devotionals/">spiritual quiet time</a>.
</p>

## Practical Ways to Practice This Today

1. **Thank the Giver directly when enjoying a gift:** When you experience a good meal, pleasant weather, or a kind conversation, pause to say: 'Thank You, Father, for this gift.'
2. **Conduct an 'open hands' prayer check:** Identify the blessing you would feel most terrified to lose, and consciously entrust it into God's sovereign care.
3. **Rest in the permanent gift of Christ:** Spend two minutes in quiet meditation, reminding your soul that though earthly things change, Jesus' presence is eternal.
