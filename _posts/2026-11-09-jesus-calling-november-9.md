## Reflection: Learning to Wait Without Wasting the Wait

In our modern culture, waiting is treated as lost time—an irritating delay between where we are and where we demand to be. Yet across Scripture, waiting is never empty space. Biblical waiting is not passive resignation, idleness, or emotional paralysis. It is an active posture of persistent prayer, patient obedience, and quiet expectation anchored in the character of a faithful God.

When the psalmist declares, *"Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD,"* he repeats the charge because our restless hearts need to hear it twice. The courage required in a prolonged wait does not look like dramatic heroics; it looks like showing up each morning, doing the ordinary duty before you, loving the people God has placed around you, and refusing to take control through frantic, self-willed shortcuts.

While you are waiting on God to move in your circumstances, God is often doing a profound work in you. He is dismantling self-reliance, rooting out hidden idols of control, and teaching you that His presence in the hallway is far more precious than merely getting through the door. The renewal Isaiah describes does not belong to the hurried or the self-sufficient, but to those who lean entirely upon their Lord.

## Try This Today: A Waiting-Season Journal

If you find yourself in an extended season of waiting, try this simple spiritual exercise:

* **Name the Waiting Room:** Open a clean page in a notebook and write down the situation or decision you are currently waiting on.
* **Daily Trace of Faithfulness:** At the close of each day, write a single sentence: *"Today God was faithful in..."* Record an answered prayer, an unexpected moment of peace, a scripture that encouraged you, or practical help received.
* **Look Back with Gratitude:** After several weeks, reread your entries. You will see how God sustained you through every quiet day, proving that He was working even when you could not yet see the final outcome.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does biblical waiting mean taking no practical action?
No. Biblical waiting is active, not passive. It means taking every faithful, responsible step you can take in alignment with God's Word, while entrusting the ultimate timing and results to Him. As we explore in our companion reflection on [Trusting When You Cannot Trace the Path](/jesus-calling-november-4/), faith does not mean turning off your mind, but placing your confidence in God rather than your own understanding.

### Why does Psalm 27:14 repeat the word "wait"?
David repeats *"wait, I say, on the LORD"* because our natural human instinct in difficulty is either to despair or to take matters into our own hands. The repetition serves as a gentle, pastoral reminder to keep our eyes fixed on God's sovereignty.
