<p class="lead">
  It is hard to listen when your mind is loud. Most people know the feeling: you sit down for a quiet moment to pray, and instead of stillness, your thoughts race through a running list of everything that could go wrong. The October 7 reading in Sarah Young's devotional *Jesus Calling* begins right at that tension. Before we can perceive God's quiet peace, we need an intentional place to lay down the worries crowding the room.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 7 About?

Jesus Calling October 7 focuses on releasing anxious worries to God so that our hearts become quiet enough to hear His voice. The devotional encourages readers to surrender daily concerns, receive each day as a gift from God, and practice gratitude in all circumstances. Anchored in 1 Peter 5:7 and Philippians 4:6–7, the practical takeaway is to write down top worries, pray over them specifically, and rest in God's sovereign care.

## Today's Theme: Releasing Cares to Cultivate Quietness

<p>
  The reading connects three vital spiritual ideas. First, unmanaged worries and runaway fears crowd our mental and spiritual space, keeping us from sensing Christ's presence. Entrusting those anxieties to Jesus is the necessary first step. Second, we are encouraged to accept each day as it arrives, recognizing that God is sovereign over today's moments rather than projecting fears into next month. Third, rather than resenting unpredictable circumstances, we practice thankfulness—a spiritual discipline that turns our focus from what is lacking to the God who sustains us.
</p>

## Bible Verses Related to Today's Theme

### Casting our cares upon Him
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">1 Peter 5:7 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 1 Peter 5:7 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The Apostle Peter does not tell believers to pretend they have no responsibilities or burdens. Rather, the command is to transfer the crushing weight onto shoulders capable of carrying it. Notice the comforting reason given: because He cares for you personally.</p>

### Receiving the day as given
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Psalm 118:24 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;This is the day which the LORD hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Psalm 118:24 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The psalmist does not claim that every hour will be easy or comfortable. He affirms that the day itself is a divine gift, provided with sufficient grace for whatever it holds.</p>

### Giving thanks in the midst of life
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">1 Thessalonians 5:18 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 1 Thessalonians 5:18 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The biblical instruction is to give thanks *in* everything, not *for* every tragedy or painful occurrence. Scripture never asks us to celebrate suffering, but invites us to acknowledge God's abiding presence inside it.</p>

## Why This Matters in Everyday Life

<p>
  Anxiety and attention constantly compete for the same inner space. The Apostle Paul describes this holy exchange in Philippians 4:6–7: when we bring our honest requests to God with thanksgiving, the peace of God, which surpasses human understanding, guards our hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. This pattern is not an emotional formula to instantly erase all stress. It is a steady, faithful discipline of placing our burdens where they safely belong.
</p>

## Practical Ways to Practice This Today

1. **Write down your concerns, then hand them over:** Jot down two or three pressing worries on paper. Pray specifically over each one, then verbally commit them into God's hands.
2. **Take five minutes of undivided stillness:** Put your phone in another room. Sit quietly without formulating petitions, allowing your racing thoughts to settle in Christ's peace.
3. **Name three specific items of gratitude:** Thank God for three concrete blessings today, including one spiritual grace discovered in the middle of a difficult situation.
