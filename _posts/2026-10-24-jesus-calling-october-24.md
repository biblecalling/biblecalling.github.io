<p class="lead">
  Within our <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/jesus-calling-october-2026/">Jesus Calling October 2026 collection</a>, smartphones buzz endlessly, inboxes overflow, and the pressure to produce never stops. In modern culture, taking time to rest often feels like an unaffordable luxury—or worse, a guilty failure. The October 24 reading in *Jesus Calling* argues that spiritual rest is neither a luxury nor a sin; it is an essential part of how God designed human beings.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 24 About?

Jesus Calling October 24 focuses on the vital necessity of unwinding in the Shepherd's presence and disconnecting from digital exhaustion. The devotional observes that constant electronic connectivity keeps people too tense to perceive God, urging believers to accept rest without guilt. Grounded in Psalm 23:1–3, Mark 6:31, and Matthew 11:28, the practical takeaway is to keep your morning phone-free and schedule an intentional hour of quiet restoration.

## The Core Spiritual Lesson: Green Pastures in a Wired World: The Holy Duty of Rest

<p>
  The reading calls believers to unwind in the gentle presence of the Good Shepherd. It observes that our electronic age keeps society perpetually 'wired,' leaving people too stressed and fragmented to sense God in their daily moments. Rest is woven into the very fabric of creation. The reading contrasts the unnecessary guilt many feel when resting with the immense energy wasted on hurried, frantic busyness, inviting us to walk paths of peace, bringing our hearts before God in <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/prayer/">prayer and meditation</a>.
</p>

## What Scripture Teaches Us

### He maketh me to lie down
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Psalm 23:1–3 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. He restoreth my soul.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Psalm 23:1–3 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>As highlighted in our <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/bible/">Bible teachings</a>, notice the divine initiative: the Shepherd *makes* His sheep lie down. Sheep only rest when they feel secure, well-fed, and free from pests. The Shepherd provides the safety that makes rest possible.</p>

### Jesus invites weary disciples to rest
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Mark 6:31 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Mark 6:31 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Jesus did not rebuke His disciples for feeling tired after ministry; He ordered them to withdraw and rest.</p>

### Rest for the weary soul
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Matthew 11:28 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Matthew 11:28 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Christ's invitation is open to all who are exhausted by religious striving and life's crushing loads.</p>

## Navigating Everyday Life With This Truth

<p>
  Spiritual rest is an active confession of faith: when you stop working, you are declaring that God is in control of the world and that His sovereignty does not depend on your nonstop labor. Constant digital connectivity trains our brains to anticipate perpetual interruption, making silence feel unnatural or threatening. Intentionally carving out quiet pockets allows your soul to catch up with your body, which we cultivate through a steady <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/devotionals/">daily quiet time rhythm</a>.
</p>

## Three Concrete Action Steps for Today

1. **Protect your first ten minutes from screens:** Wake up and read Psalm 23 or pray quietly before glancing at email, news, or social feeds.
2. **Schedule a 'green pasture' block this week:** Set aside one hour with notifications silenced to read Scripture, take a peaceful walk, or simply rest.
3. **Banish rest-guilt with truth:** When you sit down to rest, speak to your soul: 'Rest is not laziness; it is a gift from my Shepherd.'

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Jesus Calling October 24 about?
<p>Jesus Calling October 24 focuses on the vital necessity of unwinding in the Shepherd's presence and disconnecting from digital exhaustion. The devotional observes that constant electronic connectivity keeps people too tense to perceive God, urging believers to accept rest without guilt. Grounded in Psalm 23:1–3, Mark 6:31, and Matthew 11:28, the practical takeaway is to keep your morning phone-free and schedule an intentional hour of quiet restoration.</p>

### Why does Mark 6:31 highlight Jesus calling His disciples to rest?
<p>In Mark 6:31, Jesus noticed that the disciples were so busy with ministry they had no time even to eat, commanding them to come apart to a desolate place and rest. Jesus modeled that rest is a necessary rhythm of human stewardship, not a spiritual failure.</p>

### Is Jesus Calling considered biblical Scripture?
<p>No. Jesus Calling is a contemporary devotional aid. The Holy Bible is the inspired, infallible Word of God. Devotional readings are beneficial when they direct readers back to Scripture.</p>

### How can I overcome digital fatigue and rest today?
<p>Keep the first ten minutes of your morning completely phone-free, schedule an intentional quiet hour with notifications silenced, and reject feelings of guilt by remembering that rest is a gift from the Shepherd.</p>
