<p class="lead">
  Few experiences cut deeper than being treated unfairly: being misunderstood, slandered, betrayed, or cheated. The October 28 reading in *Jesus Calling* addresses unfair treatment head-on, offering a surprising and deeply liberating source of comfort.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 28 About?

Jesus Calling October 28 focuses on responding to unfair treatment with quick forgiveness and finding comfort in Christ's robe of righteousness rather than fighting to defend our reputation. The devotional reminds believers that earthly life is not fair, but God's grace in justifying us was not 'fair' either—it was unmerited mercy. Supported by Colossians 3:13, Isaiah 61:10, and Romans 12:19, the practical takeaway is to release bitterness to God, set wise boundaries, and rest in His view of you.

## The Core Spiritual Lesson: Forgiveness When Life Isn't Fair: Clothed in Grace

<p>
  The reading counsels believers not to expect earthly life to be consistently fair; people will sometimes hurt us without cause. When mistreatment occurs, the reading suggests viewing it as a classroom to grow in Christlike grace: forgiving quickly rather than fighting frantically to set the record straight. It urges us to focus on Jesus' evaluation of our life, reminding us that we are clothed in His spotless robe of righteousness—a gift that was pure, unearned mercy.
</p>

## What Scripture Teaches Us

### Forgiving as Christ forgave
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Colossians 3:13 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Colossians 3:13 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The standard for Christian forgiveness is not whether the offender deserves mercy, but how generously Christ forgave us at the cross.</p>

### Clothed in the robe of righteousness
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Isaiah 61:10 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Isaiah 61:10 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Our standing before God is not woven from our own moral perfection or reputation; it is an imputed garment of Christ's perfect righteousness.</p>

### Vengeance belongs to God
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Romans 12:19 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Romans 12:19 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Releasing vengeance to God frees us from carrying the toxic torch of bitterness, trusting that the Judge of all the earth will do right.</p>

## Navigating Everyday Life With This Truth

<p>
  Forgiveness is often dangerously misunderstood: it does not mean pretending abuse never occurred, and it does not mean remaining in an unsafe environment. Forgiving someone releases our claim for personal vengeance into God's hands. Setting healthy boundaries, having honest conversations, and reporting criminal behavior or abuse to appropriate authorities are fully biblical and necessary actions. The ultimate peace is knowing that human slander cannot stain the righteousness Christ has placed upon you.
</p>

## Three Concrete Action Steps for Today

1. **Name the hurt and release the debt:** Honestly describe the mistreatment to God, then verbally pray: 'Lord, I release my claim for revenge into Your hands.'
2. **Step away from defending your ego:** Resist the urge to fire off an angry counterattack or gossip to win allies.
3. **Establish healthy boundaries where needed:** Consult a wise pastor or Christian counselor if dealing with recurring toxicity or mistreatment.

### What is Jesus Calling October 28 about?

The devotional reminds believers that earthly life is not fair, but God's grace in justifying us was not 'fair' either—it was unmerited mercy. ### How does Colossians 3:13 ground the Christian call to forgive?
<p>Colossians 3:13 commands believers to forgive one another just as the Lord forgave them. Christian forgiveness is not based on the offender's worthiness, but on the overwhelming mercy Christ demonstrated toward us at the cross.</p>

### Is Jesus Calling equal in authority to the Holy Bible?
<p>No. Only the Holy Bible is divinely inspired Scripture. Jesus Calling is a devotional work written to encourage personal prayer and reflection, but it holds no biblical authority of its own.</p>

### What is the difference between biblical forgiveness and tolerating mistreatment?
<p>Forgiveness releases personal vengeance and bitterness into God's hands. It does not require remaining in an unsafe environment, ignoring boundaries, or failing to report abuse. Seeking safety and counsel are wise, biblical actions alongside forgiveness.</p>
