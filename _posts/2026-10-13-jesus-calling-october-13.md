<p class="lead">
  When your calendar is packed and notifications never stop, stepping away for quiet reflection feels like the last thing you can afford. Yet the October 13 reading in *Jesus Calling* suggests that the more overwhelmed you feel, the more vital holy stillness becomes.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 13 About?

Jesus Calling October 13 centers on the necessity of pausing in God's presence to receive the deep, durable peace that only Christ can provide. The reading encourages slowing down, breathing deeply, and receiving the light of God's countenance rather than rushing through the day on empty. Anchored in Psalm 46:10, Numbers 6:25–26, and John 14:27, the practical takeaway is to practice a five-minute pause of intentional stillness today.

## Understanding Today's Focus: Stillness in the Rush: Receiving the Peace of Christ

<p>
  Sarah Young's devotional prompts believers to pause before God, pointing out that when we feel most frazzled, quiet communion is not a luxury—it is spiritual oxygen. Pausing to rest under the light of Jesus' countenance allows us to receive the supernatural peace He purchased for us at great cost. This peace is not dependent on circumstances remaining calm; it is an internal anchor that steadies our minds regardless of outer storms.
</p>

## Biblical Anchor & Passages

### Knowing God in stillness
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Psalm 46:10 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Psalm 46:10 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The Hebrew root for 'be still' conveys letting go, ceasing striving, and slacking your grip. God calls us to stop trying to manage the universe and remember who sits on the throne.</p>

### The blessing of His countenance
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Numbers 6:25–26 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Numbers 6:25–26 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>The Aaronic blessing reveals God's tender posture toward His people: His face shines with grace, lifting up His countenance to impart genuine peace.</p>

### Peace not as the world gives
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">John 14:27 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; John 14:27 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Worldly peace depends on favorable circumstances, financial surplus, and absence of conflict. Christ's peace is His very presence dwelling within us in the storm.</p>

## Living With Perspective & Grace

<p>
  The peace Jesus gives is not merely an emotional calm; it is a spiritual reality grounded in reconciliation with God. Taking a few intentional minutes of quiet does not instantly make all difficult deadlines disappear, but it reorients our perspective. When we start from resting in God's presence, we face our obligations with clarity, patience, and supernatural endurance.
</p>

## How to Put This Into Practice Today

1. **Take an intentional five-minute stillness break:** Silence your digital devices, sit in an upright, relaxed posture, and take slow, deep breaths.
2. **Use a simple breath prayer:** On each inhale, pray silently: 'Lord Jesus,' and on each exhale: 'fill me with Your peace.'
3. **Read Numbers 6:24–26 over your day:** Treat the Aaronic blessing as God's personal word of favor spoken directly over your soul.

### What does Psalm 46:10 mean by 'Be still, and know that I am God'?
<p>In Psalm 46:10, the Hebrew phrasing for 'be still' implies slacking one's grip, ceasing striving, and letting go of frantic control. It is an invitation to pause amidst life's chaos and recognize that God reigns sovereignly over all circumstances.</p>

### Is Jesus Calling considered Scripture?
<p>No. Jesus Calling is a devotional book by Sarah Young. Scripture refers exclusively to the Holy Bible, which is the unique, God-breathed standard for Christian truth and doctrine.</p>
