## Quick Answer

This companion reflection for **Jesus Calling November 12** explores the invitation to seek divine discernment without hesitation or embarrassment. James promises that God gives wisdom generously to all who ask, without scolding us for our lack of understanding.

  > "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him."

* **Psalm 32:8 (KJV):**  
  > "I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye."

## Reflection: Asking God for Wisdom

The phrase *"and upbraideth not"* in James 1:5 is among the most comforting characterizations of God in the entire Bible. It means our Heavenly Father never rolls His eyes at our confusion, never sighs at our ignorance, and never reminds us of how many times we have made foolish missteps in the past. When we approach Him acknowledging our lack of wisdom, He gives *liberally*—with open, gracious hands.

In Psalm 32:8, the Lord promises, *"I will guide thee with mine eye."* That picture speaks of intimate proximity. A guide does not need to shout across a crowded room if you are walking right beside Him; a quiet look or a gentle inclination is enough. When we walk closely with the Lord, guidance rarely arrives as a booming earthquake; it usually unfolds as clear moral conviction, sound thinking, and the quiet counsel of trusted believers.

When facing complicated life choices—careers, relationships, finances, or family transitions—many believers look exclusively for an emotional feeling of "peace." While inner calm can be a blessing, emotional tranquility alone is not foolproof proof that a direction is right, nor does lingering anxiety prove a choice is wrong. Difficult, faithful choices—like confronting sin or taking a costly stand for truth—frequently feel emotionally unsettling at the start. True wisdom is anchored in Scripture, tested by wise counsel, and walked out in humble obedience.

## Try This Today: A Grounded 5-Question Decision Checklist

When you face an important decision, examine your path through this balanced, biblical framework:

1. **Scriptural Consistency:** Does this proposed path align completely with the clear teaching and moral commands of Scripture?
2. **Love of God and Neighbor:** Does this choice genuinely promote love for God, selfless service to others, and integrity, rather than mere self-preservation?
3. **Wise and Trustworthy Counsel:** What do mature, grounded Christians who know your blind spots advise? As we discussed in [Trusting When You Cannot Trace the Path](/jesus-calling-november-4/), *"where no counsel is, the people fall"* (Proverbs 11:14).
4. **Evidence and Consequences:** Have you looked honestly at the facts, practical responsibilities, and realistic long-term outcomes of this choice?
5. **The Next Faithful Step:** Rather than trying to map the next ten years, what is the single next responsible, obedient step God is asking you to take today?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does feeling nervous or unsettled mean I am making the wrong decision?
Not necessarily. Difficult, courageous, and righteous decisions (such as setting healthy boundaries, confessing a wrong, or taking a sacrificial step of faith) often cause natural human nervousness. Do not rely solely on emotional feelings as your guide; test the decision against God's Word, practical facts, and godly counsel.

### How does James 1:5 define biblical wisdom?
Biblical wisdom is not merely high intellect or book knowledge; it is the moral skill and spiritual understanding required to live righteously and make decisions that honor God in an imperfect, fallen world.
