Worry is one of the most universal experiences of human life. We worry about finances, health, our children's futures, changing circumstances, and things that may never come to pass. Yet in the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus spoke directly to this deep human struggle with words that are as radical and comforting today as when He first spoke them beside the Sea of Galilee.

In Matthew 6:25–34, Jesus does not dismiss worry as trivial, nor does He scold His listeners for feeling vulnerable. Instead, He gently reorients our perspective toward the faithful character of God.

## What Jesus Teaches in the Sermon on the Mount

Jesus begins His teaching on anxiety with a straightforward command:

<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Words of Jesus</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Therefore I tell you, do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat or what you will drink, nor about your body, what you will put on. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothing?&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Matthew 6:25</div>
</div>

Jesus uses two vivid everyday illustrations from the natural world to illustrate God's tender care: the birds of the air and the lilies of the field.

### 1. The Birds of the Air
Jesus points out that birds do not sow, reap, or store away in barns, yet our Heavenly Father feeds them. He then asks a penetrating question: *&ldquo;Are you not of more value than they?&rdquo;* (Matthew 6:26). 

Worry often stems from a subconscious fear that God has forgotten us. Jesus reminds us that the Creator who oversees every sparrow is our Father, and His love for us is infinitely greater.

### 2. The Lilies of the Field
Jesus directs our attention to the wild flowers growing across the Galilean hillsides. Even King Solomon in all his royal splendor was not clothed like one of these. If God clothes grass that is here today and gone tomorrow, how much more will He care for His children?

## The Ineffectiveness of Worry

Jesus adds a profound practical insight in Matthew 6:27: *&ldquo;Which of you by being anxious can add a single hour to his span of life?&rdquo;*

Worry produces internal turmoil without solving the problem. It consumes today's emotional energy anticipating tomorrow's challenges. As the Christian writer Corrie ten Boom famously observed, worry does not empty tomorrow of its sorrow; it empties today of its strength.

## How to Apply Christ's Words to Everyday Life

When anxiety tightens your chest and racing thoughts keep you awake, how can you practice Jesus' teaching?

1. **Acknowledge the Worry Honestly:** Bring your specific fears into the light. Name the concern before God rather than suppressing it.
2. **Shift Your Focus from the Problem to the Provider:** Remind yourself of who God is. If He sustains the universe, He is capable of walking with you through your current difficulty.
3. **Seek First His Kingdom:** In Matthew 6:33, Jesus provides the ultimate antidote: *&ldquo;Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.&rdquo;* Focus on honoring Christ in this present moment, trusting Him with the outcome.
4. **Live One Day at a Time:** *&ldquo;Do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble&rdquo;* (Matthew 6:34). God gives daily bread and daily grace—not weekly or monthly grace in advance.

## Bible Verses to Remember

* **Philippians 4:6–7:** &ldquo;Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.&rdquo;
* **1 Peter 5:7:** &ldquo;Casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you.&rdquo;
* **Psalm 56:3:** &ldquo;When I am afraid, I put my trust in you.&rdquo;
* **Isaiah 26:3:** &ldquo;You keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts in you.&rdquo;

## Conclusion

Overcoming worry is not a one-time achievement, but a daily rhythm of surrender. Each time anxiety knocks at your door, let it serve as a prompt to run into the presence of Jesus, resting securely in your Father's unfailing care.
