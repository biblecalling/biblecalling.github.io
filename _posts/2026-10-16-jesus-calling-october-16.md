<p class="lead">
  We usually think of comfort as a personal blessing—something we receive from God to soothe our own grief or stress. The October 16 reading in *Jesus Calling* reveals a beautiful second dimension: the comfort we receive from Christ is meant to flow through us to heal others.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 16 About?

Jesus Calling October 16 focuses on continually looking to Jesus for comfort and companionship, and allowing the comfort we receive to overflow into the lives of others who are hurting. The reading emphasizes that Christ's fellowship is the greatest treasure of salvation, one that cannot be stripped away by earthly loss. Grounded in 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 and Psalm 34:5, the practical takeaway is to receive God's mercy first, then reach out to comfort one person today.

## The Core Spiritual Lesson: Comforted by God to Comfort Others

<p>
  Believers are urged to look toward Jesus continually throughout the day for strength, counsel, and companionship. Even a momentary prayer or brief upward glance reconnects our awareness with Him. When we face trials, Jesus enfolds us in His tender care. But divine comfort is not meant to stagnate within us; as we experience God's mercy in our own weakness, we become gentle instruments through which He comforts other struggling souls.
</p>

## What Scripture Teaches Us

### Looking to Him brings light
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Psalm 34:5 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;They looked unto him, and were lightened: and their faces were not ashamed.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Psalm 34:5 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Turning our eyes toward God brings spiritual clarity and removes the paralyzing shame that isolates weary hearts.</p>

### Seeking His face continually
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">1 Chronicles 16:11 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Seek the LORD and his strength, seek his face continually.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 1 Chronicles 16:11 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>True spiritual stamina does not come from stockpiling past experiences; it comes from ongoing, daily communion with the living Lord.</p>

### The Father of all mercies
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">2 Corinthians 1:3–4 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Blessed be God... the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Paul reveals the holy pipeline of grace: our painful tribulations become the very classroom where we learn how to comfort others with authenticity.</p>

## Navigating Everyday Life With This Truth

<p>
  Our most difficult life seasons often yield our greatest ministry to others. A person who has experienced grief, depression, or severe disappointment and tasted God's comfort is uniquely equipped to sit with another hurting person—not with simplistic clichés, but with quiet, compassionate understanding. Serving as a channel of comfort also protects us from becoming self-absorbed in our own trials.
</p>

## Three Concrete Action Steps for Today

1. **Practice the brief upward glance:** When stress spikes during your workday, pause for two seconds and pray: 'Lord Jesus, I look to You.'
2. **Receive before attempting to give:** Spend quiet time letting God's promises comfort your heart so you are not serving others out of spiritual exhaustion.
3. **Send an encouraging message to one person:** Reach out via text, card, or phone call to someone walking through a difficult trial, letting them know they are prayed for.
