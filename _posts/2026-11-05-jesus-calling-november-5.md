## Quick Answer: What Is Jesus Calling November 5 About?

This companion reflection for Jesus Calling November 5 shows how to turn chronic worry into active prayer. The Apostle Peter instructs believers to cast their cares upon God because He cares for them, while Paul reveals how coupling requests with thanksgiving releases peace into anxious minds.

## Reflection: Handing Over the Worry List

"Casting" is a deliberate throwing word. You do not place a heavy weight tentatively on a stranger's back; you toss it to someone strong enough to catch it. Peter gives the underlying reason right inside the command: *for He careth for you*. We are not imposing upon a distracted or reluctant God; we are handing our cares over to a Father who is already watching over us with infinite tenderness.

Paul's pattern in Philippians 4 contains three vital steps: naming the issue honestly (prayer), making specific requests (supplication), and recalling God's past goodness (thanksgiving). Gratitude does not pretend problems are trivial; it reminds us who carried us through previous trials. Experiencing <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/jesus-calling-november-3/">perfect peace in uncertain times</a> begins when we recognize which concerns belong in God's hands.

## Try This Today: The Worry-Handoff Exercise

1. **Categorize your concerns:** Write down your current worries. Next to each, mark whether it is:
   * **Mine to act on:** A concrete responsibility where you can take a practical, healthy step today.
   * **Mine to release:** A circumstance outside your control (another person's choices, future hypotheticals) that belongs entirely to God.
   * **Both:** A matter requiring both prudent effort and ongoing prayer.
2. **Pray the release:** For the items you cannot control, pray aloud: *"Lord Jesus, I place this into Your hands. I choose not to carry it as my burden today."*
3. **Add thanksgiving:** Write down one specific blessing or past answered prayer next to every concern on your list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is experiencing anxiety a sin or spiritual defect?

No. Scripture treats anxious people with gentleness and understanding (1 Kings 19:5–7; Psalm 94:19). Human vulnerability is part of living in a fallen world. If worry returns, it is an invitation to pray again rather than a sign of failure. However, if anxiety is persistent, debilitating, or interferes with your health and daily functioning, reaching out to a licensed counselor or medical doctor alongside your prayer life is a wise, responsible, and healthy choice.

### What should I do when I cannot solve a problem through action?

Recognize the boundary between human responsibility and divine sovereignty. Fulfill whatever small, ethical duties you have, and surrender the outcome to God in prayer.
