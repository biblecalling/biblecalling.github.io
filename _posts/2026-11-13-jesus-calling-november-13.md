## Quick Answer

This companion reflection for **Jesus Calling November 13** meets Friday the 13th with a calming spiritual question: Do our lives depend on random luck, omens, and unpredictable fate, or are they cradled in the hands of a sovereign Heavenly Father? Scripture answers clearly: our times are securely in God's hands.

  > "My times are in thy hand: deliver me from the hand of mine enemies, and from them that persecute me."

* **Romans 8:28 (KJV):**  
  > "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

## Reflection: Not Luck, But a Father Who Holds Your Times

Superstitions tend to flourish in places where human beings feel vulnerable and powerless. When the future feels uncertain or frightening, people often cling to charms, avoidance routines, or ominous calendar dates in an effort to manage risk. Scripture does not ridicule that vulnerability; instead, it offers something infinitely firmer than chance.

David wrote the words of Psalm 31 while surrounded by genuine danger, slander, and military threats. Yet in verse 15, he breathes four steadying words: *"My times are in thy hand."* Not in the grip of blind fate, not subject to astrological signs, and not vulnerable to arbitrary dates on a calendar. Every season, every breath, and every day of your life is known, guarded, and sustained by the Creator.

Romans 8:28 brings profound clarity to this truth. The apostle Paul does not claim that every event in this fallen world is intrinsically good; tragedy, pain, and loss are real and grievous. What Paul promises is that God is actively working *through* all things—even the heartbreaks, delays, and unexpected turns—to bring about ultimate spiritual good and conformity to Christ for those who love Him. Friday the 13th is simply another day that the Lord has made (Psalm 118:24). You can walk through it with joyful confidence, knowing the Shepherd walks beside you.

## Try This Today

* **Replace Superstition with Scripture:** Whenever an anxious thought arises suggesting that something bad is bound to happen, intentionally reply aloud: *"My times are in God's hands."*
* **Meditate on Psalm 31:** Read Psalm 31:14–16 slowly and prayerfully, letting the certainty of God's steadfast love quiet lingering fears.
* **Cultivate Joy and Generosity:** Defy the gloomy folklore of Friday the 13th by doing something cheerful and generous: bake a treat for coworkers, send an encouraging note, or bless a neighbor in need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does the Bible say about luck and superstition?
Scripture consistently directs believers away from relying on omens, charms, and fatalism (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). The Bible teaches that God's loving providence, rather than arbitrary fortune, governs all creation. Believers are called to trust in His character rather than fear superstitious symbols.

### Does Romans 8:28 mean that bad things are secretly good things?
No. Scripture never calls evil good or good evil (Isaiah 5:20). Sickness, injustice, and death are consequences of a fallen world. Romans 8:28 promises that God's redemptive power is greater than evil, meaning He can take even painful circumstances and weave them into eternal purpose and spiritual maturity.
