<p class="lead">
  Resentment is one of the stealthiest spiritual poisons. It rarely announces its arrival with trumpets; it creeps in as quiet irritation when our plans get derailed, or as a persistent grudge when life does not unfold as we expected. The October 21 reading in *Jesus Calling* invites us to bring these hidden resentments into God's light.
</p>

## What Is Jesus Calling October 21 About?

Jesus Calling October 21 centers on identifying hidden resentment and rebellious attitudes toward God when circumstances disappoint our expectations. The reading emphasizes bringing these painful feelings honestly to Christ and responding with humble submission and gratitude rather than bitter resistance. Grounded in Job 1:21, Hebrews 12:15, and 1 Peter 5:6, the practical takeaway is to name your resentments in prayer, honor honest grief, and hold blessings loosely.

## Understanding Today's Focus: Surrendering Resentment at the Feet of Christ

<p>
  The reading points out that walking consistently in Christ's presence requires confronting our internal resistance, especially the resentment we feel when life interferes with our personal desires. Pushing those feelings underground only allows bitterness to fester. The spiritual path forward is to let them surface before Jesus, submit to His loving authority, and practice gratitude, recognizing that everything we possess is an unearned gift from His hand.
</p>

## Biblical Anchor & Passages

### Job's posture of worship amid loss
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Job 1:21 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;The LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Job 1:21 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Notice Job's profound context: before speaking these words, Job tore his robe and fell to the ground in deep grief (Job 1:20). He did not deny pain; he brought broken worship into the loss.</p>

### Guarding against the root of bitterness
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">Hebrews 12:15 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; Hebrews 12:15 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Bitterness is compared to an invasive root: if left unaddressed, it silently spreads underground, poisoning our relationships and spiritual vitality.</p>

### Humbling oneself under God's hand
<div class="scripture-highlight">
  <div class="scripture-highlight-title">1 Peter 5:6 (KJV)</div>
  <blockquote class="scripture-highlight-text">
    &ldquo;Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time.&rdquo;
  </blockquote>
  <div class="scripture-highlight-ref">&mdash; 1 Peter 5:6 (KJV)</div>
</div>
<p>Submitting to God's hand is not admitting defeat to an uncaring tyrant; it is yielding our lives to a loving Father who lifts us up in His perfect time.</p>

## Living With Perspective & Grace

<p>
  It is critically important to distinguish between honest grief and destructive bitterness. Grieving a loss, mourning a missed opportunity, or weeping over a heartbreak is not rebellion against God; Jesus wept openly at the grave of Lazarus. The devotional is not commanding you to skip sorrow. It is warning against letting unresolved disappointment curdle into cold bitterness against God or other people. God can handle your tears, but He calls you to release your grudges.
</p>

## How to Put This Into Practice Today

1. **Name one specific resentment in prayer:** Identify the exact situation or person you feel bitter toward, and speak it plainly before the Lord.
2. **Distinguish grief from grudge:** Give yourself permission to mourn what was lost without blaming God or harboring vengeance.
3. **Express gratitude for one overlooked gift:** Actively thank God for one blessing you have taken for granted, breaking the cycle of entitlement.
